Fear rarely makes an appointment. It arrives with a test result, a phone call from the school, a strange noise at night, or a bank balance that doesn’t add up. Your heart speeds up before your mind has even caught up with what happened.

You may have heard that “fear not” appears 365 times in the Bible, one for every day of the year. It’s a lovely idea, but it isn’t true: the real count depends on the translation and is well under that. God doesn’t hand out a ration of comfort per day, though. Whenever His people were afraid, He came close and spoke to them, and He still does.

Here are eleven verses to keep within reach. Read them out loud if you can. Fear is loud, and it helps to answer it with your own voice.

## When fear hits suddenly

**1. Psalm 56:3.** “What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee.” David wrote this while he was in enemy hands. He doesn’t say *if* I am afraid. He assumes he will be, and decides ahead of time where he’ll run.

**2. Isaiah 41:10.** “Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

**3. Mark 6:50.** “Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid.” Jesus said this to His friends in a boat at night, when they saw Him walking on the water and thought He was a ghost. Sometimes the thing that frightens us is the very place He is coming toward us.

## When you have to walk into something hard

**4. Joshua 1:9.** “Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.”

**5. Psalm 23:4.** “Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.” Notice that the psalm goes *through* the valley. It doesn’t promise a way around it.

**6. Psalm 27:1.** “The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? the LORD is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?”

## When fear tells you who you are

**7. Isaiah 43:1.** “Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

**8. 2 Timothy 1:7.** “For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.” Paul wrote this to Timothy, a young pastor who seems to have struggled with timidity. Fear may visit you, but it didn’t come from God, and it doesn’t get to define you.

**9. 1 John 4:18.** “There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear.” The more you understand how loved you are, the less room fear has to work with.

## When you need peace to stay

**10. John 14:27.** “Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.” Jesus said this the night before the cross, to men who were about to watch everything fall apart.

**11. Psalm 34:4.** “I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.”

## What to do with these verses

Pick the one that fits the fear you’re carrying right now, and write it somewhere you’ll see it: a sticky note on the mirror, the lock screen of your phone, the inside of your car visor. When the fear comes back (it usually does), you won’t have to go looking for an answer.

Then tell God plainly what you’re afraid of. He already knows, but saying it to Him moves the fear out of the dark and into a conversation. David did this over and over in the Psalms, and he was one of the bravest men in Scripture.

If fear has started to run your days, keeping you from sleeping, working or leaving the house, please talk to your doctor or a Christian counselor. Asking for help is one of the ways God brings people close to you.
