If you’ve ever searched “Bible verses about anxiety” at two in the morning, you’re not alone. And if some of the answers you found left you feeling more guilty than comforted, you’re not alone in that either.

Many Christian women carry a quiet fear underneath their anxiety: *If I really trusted God, I wouldn’t feel like this.* So let’s look at what Scripture actually says, and what it doesn’t.

## 1. The Bible never pretends God’s people don’t struggle

Open the Psalms and you’ll find people who are deeply honest about distress. “Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me?” (Psalm 42:11). David, Elijah, Hannah and Jeremiah all went through seasons of deep heaviness.

Even Jesus, in the garden of Gethsemane, said, “My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death” (Mark 14:34). The Son of God brought His distress straight to His Father.

So feeling anxious does not mean your faith is broken. You are human, and you are in very good company.

## 2. “Be careful for nothing” is an invitation, not an accusation

Philippians 4:6 is probably the most quoted verse on anxiety: “Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.”

Paul doesn’t say, “If you worry, you’re sinning.” He gives you something to do instead: take the thing you’re anxious about and turn it into a prayer, be specific, add thanksgiving, and hand it over.

And then comes the promise: “the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus” (Philippians 4:7). The word *keep* is a military word that describes soldiers standing guard, so God’s peace protects you as well as calming you.

## 3. God invites you to throw your worries, not hide them

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you” (1 Peter 5:7). The word for *casting* is used only one other time in the New Testament: when the disciples threw their cloaks onto a colt for Jesus to ride into Jerusalem. It’s a deliberate, physical act.

You will probably have to cast the same worry more than once, and doing it again is practice, not failure.

## 4. Anxiety is not the whole story about you

Jesus said, “In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world” (John 16:33). Jesus admits the trouble is real and says He has already overcome it. Your anxious thoughts may be loud, but they are not in charge.

Hebrews 6:19 describes our hope in Christ as “an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast.” An anchor won’t stop a storm, but it keeps the boat from being carried away.

## What the Bible doesn’t say

- **It doesn’t say anxiety means God is angry with you.** Again and again, He meets overwhelmed people with tenderness: bread and water for Elijah, a God who saw Hagar in the desert, an outstretched hand for Peter.
- **It doesn’t say you must face it alone.** Again and again in Scripture, God’s people lean on each other. “Bear ye one another’s burdens” (Galatians 6:2).
- **It doesn’t say getting help is a lack of faith.** Luke was a physician. Paul told Timothy to take a little wine for his stomach. Seeing a doctor or counselor can be one of the ways God cares for you.

## A simple practice for today

Take a piece of paper and write down the three things weighing on you most. Next to each one, write a one-sentence prayer: *“Father, I give You ___. Thank You for ___.”* Then put the paper in your Bible as a reminder that you handed them over.
