Maybe you’ve bought a beautiful notebook with every intention of filling it with prayers, and it’s still sitting on your nightstand, mostly blank. You’re not the only one.

A prayer journal isn’t about perfect handwriting or long entries. It’s simply a place to talk with God on paper, and to remember how He answers. Here’s how to begin.

## Why write your prayers?

**It slows a racing mind.** Writing forces you to finish a thought before starting the next one. For many people, that alone brings a sense of calm.

**It helps you remember.** Over and over in Scripture, God tells His people to remember what He has done. Joshua set up twelve stones by the Jordan “that this may be a sign among you” (Joshua 4:6). A prayer journal is your own pile of memorial stones.

**It reveals patterns.** When you look back over a few months, you start to see what God was doing, what you kept worrying about, and how many prayers He answered quietly.

## A simple structure: A.C.T.S.

If you don’t know where to start, try this classic four-part pattern. Spend just a few sentences on each.

1. **Adoration.** Praise God for who He is. *“Lord, You are faithful. You never change.”* (Psalm 145 is a beautiful place to borrow words.)
2. **Confession.** Be honest about where you’ve fallen short. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9).
3. **Thanksgiving.** Name three specific things you’re grateful for today. Specific is better than general.
4. **Supplication.** Bring your requests for yourself and others. “Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you” (Matthew 7:7).

## Four prompts for tomorrow morning

- *What is the first thing on my mind today, and what would it look like to hand it to God?*
- *Which verse did I read today, and what does it tell me about who God is?*
- *Who is one person I can pray for by name today?*
- *Where did I see God’s kindness yesterday?*

## Keep an answered-prayer list

Draw a line down the back page of your journal. On one side, write the date and the request. On the other, write how and when God answered. Some answers will be “yes,” some “not yet,” and some “I have something better.” All of them will build your faith.

## Tips that help it stick

- **Tie it to something you already do,** like your first cup of coffee.
- **Keep it short.** Five minutes a day beats an hour once a month.
- **Don’t start over when you miss a day.** Just open to the next page. His mercies are new every morning (Lamentations 3:22–23).
