Anxiety has a cruel way of showing up right when you sit down to pray. You close your eyes, start with “Dear God,” and three seconds later you’re back inside the worry, running the same loop you’ve run all day. Then comes the guilt: *I can’t even pray properly.*

If that’s you, I want you to know that God is not grading your prayers. Some of the most honest prayers in the Bible are short, messy and desperate, and God answered them.

## Start where you actually are

Hannah prayed so hard in the temple that the priest thought she was drunk. Her answer to him is one of my favorite lines in Scripture: “I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit: I have drunken neither wine nor strong drink, but have poured out my soul before the LORD” (1 Samuel 1:15).

She didn’t tidy up her feelings first. She poured them out. The Psalms say the same thing to all of us: “Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us” (Psalm 62:8).

So begin by telling Him the truth, even if it sounds like this: *Lord, I’m scared about the biopsy and I can’t stop thinking about it.* That counts as prayer.

## Use the shortest prayer you know

When Peter started sinking in the waves, he didn’t have time for a long prayer. He “cried, saying, Lord, save me” (Matthew 14:30). The next verse says that Jesus “immediately” stretched out His hand and caught him.

When anxiety is loud, a short prayer you can repeat is often better than a long one you can’t finish. Pray *Lord, save me*, or *Jesus, help*, or just His name. Say it slowly, in time with your breathing, until your body starts to calm down a little.

## Let the Spirit carry what you can’t say

Some days you won’t have any words at all. Scripture expected that. “Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered” (Romans 8:26).

You can sit in silence before God, or cry, or sigh, and the Holy Spirit takes that to the Father as prayer.

## Borrow someone else’s words

When your own thoughts are scattered, pray a psalm. Open to Psalm 23, 46 or 121 and read it slowly to God, changing *he* to *You* as you go: *“You are my shepherd. You make me lie down…”* You’ll be praying words that have steadied anxious believers for thousands of years.

One verse I come back to often is Psalm 94:19: “In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul.” The psalmist doesn’t pretend the thoughts are gone. He finds God’s comfort right in the middle of them.

## Hand it over, then add one thank-you

Peter wrote, “Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you” (1 Peter 5:7). Name each worry, one at a time, and picture yourself placing it in God’s hands. If you pick one back up five minutes later, put it down again. Most of us have to do this many times a day.

Then thank Him for one specific thing, however small: the coffee, a text from a friend, the fact that you made it through yesterday. The problem is still there, but thanking Him reminds your anxious mind that God has been faithful before.

## A simple pattern to try tonight

1. Tell Him exactly what you’re worried about.
2. Repeat *Lord, save me* or *Jesus, help* a few times, slowly.
3. Read one psalm to Him out loud.
4. Hand over each worry by name.
5. Thank Him for one thing, and then let yourself rest.

Prayer doesn’t always make anxiety vanish, and if your anxiety is constant or overwhelming, please also talk to a doctor or a counselor. But you will never be turned away for praying badly. The God who caught Peter in the waves is listening to you right now.
