If you’re reading this because someone you love has died, I’m so sorry. You might be reading in the middle of the night, or in a parking lot after the funeral, or months later when everyone else seems to have moved on. Wherever you are, you don’t have to be strong right now.

Grief is not a lack of faith. When Jesus stood at the grave of His friend Lazarus, He “groaned in the spirit, and was troubled” (John 11:33). Then comes the shortest verse in the English Bible: “Jesus wept” (John 11:35). He cried even though He knew He was about to raise Lazarus from the dead. He didn’t hurry anyone past their sorrow, and He won’t hurry you.

Here are verses for different moments of grief. You don’t need to read them all. Find the one you need today.

## In the first raw days

**1. Psalm 34:18.** “The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

**2. Matthew 5:4.** “Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.” Jesus doesn’t say mourners will be fixed or hurried along. He says they will be comforted.

**3. Psalm 147:3.** “He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.”

## When it feels like no one understands

**4. Isaiah 53:3.** “A man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief.” Isaiah wrote this about the Messiah. Jesus knows grief from the inside.

**5. Psalm 56:8.** “Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?” God has kept track of every tear you’ve cried.

**6. John 14:16.** “And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever.” Jesus gave the Holy Spirit the name Comforter, and promised He would stay.

## When you wonder if it’s okay to still be sad

**7. Ecclesiastes 3:4.** “A time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance.” There is a season for mourning, and nobody else gets to decide how long yours lasts.

**8. 2 Corinthians 1:3–4.** “The Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation.”

## When you need hope for what comes next

**9. 1 Thessalonians 4:13.** Paul wrote to grieving believers “that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.” He doesn’t tell them not to grieve. He tells them that for those who belong to Christ, grief has hope underneath it.

**10. Revelation 21:4.** “And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

## Small ways to care for yourself while you grieve

When someone says “let me know if you need anything,” tell them something specific: a meal on Thursday, a ride, someone to sit with you on Sunday afternoon.

Birthdays, anniversaries and holidays can bring grief back with surprising force, even years later. Plan something gentle for those days, and tell a friend they’re coming.

You can tell God you’re angry, numb or confused. Many of the Psalms do exactly that.

If grief is keeping you from eating, sleeping or getting through the day for a long stretch, please talk to your pastor, a grief counselor or your doctor. Grief support groups, often run by churches and hospices, can also help a great deal.

The God who wept at Lazarus’s tomb is close to you now. You can bring Him every tear.
